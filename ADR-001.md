# ADR-001: The oracle is on-platform only

**Status:** accepted
**Date:** 2026-05-23

## Context

Daman is a slash-bonded copy-trading protocol. A leader's bond is collateral against degraded performance: if a leader trades outside the allowed universe, takes shorts, or otherwise breaches the platform's structural rules, a watchdog can attest degradation, an arbiter can rule, and a portion of the bond is slashed.

The question is what data the watchdog and the on-chain contract treat as authoritative when judging leader behavior. Two seams exist:

**Seam A: on-platform events only.** The operator-side oracle reads `TradeExecuted` and `SettlementCompleted` events that this contract itself emitted, in response to trades the operator executed on behalf of the leader. The chain is closed-world over its own state. No external feed informs the bond.

**Seam B: hybrid feeds.** The oracle ingests external leaderboards, third-party trader-performance feeds, off-platform PnL aggregators, or copy-trading-venue signals as additional input. The chain has a richer picture of leader behavior, including reputation accumulated elsewhere.

## Decision

Seam A. The oracle is on-platform only.

## Why

Seam B is recruitment-rich but oracle-corrupting. External leaderboards aggregate behavior the platform does not constrain: leverage, shorts, futures, options, instruments the screening rules deliberately exclude. Bringing those signals onto the oracle path imports their semantics into the bond's judgment. A leader who is performant under permissionless-derivatives semantics is not necessarily performant under spot-only, long-only, screened-universe semantics. The bond cannot be slashed for breaching rules the oracle was never able to observe.

Seam A is narrower but coherent. The oracle judges leaders against exactly the constraints the platform enforces, because both the constraints and the observations live in the same contract. Oracle integrity becomes a property of the architecture, not a property of how diligently the operator filters external feeds.

External leaderboards remain useful as recruitment signals on the human side. Operators may scout leaders from any source; the bond just does not read those sources as truth.

## Consequences

The oracle binary (`damanfi/oracle`) subscribes only to topics emitted by the deployment's own `IDamanCopyBond` contract address. Other event sources are not read.

The vanilla copy-bond implementation gates `recordTrade` and `recordSettlement` to the configured `oracle` address. `recordTrade` further rejects shorts (`ShortNotPermitted`) and assets not on the configured `IUniverseWhitelist` (`AssetNotEligible`). The two gates are the structural-cleanliness guarantee at the recording layer.

Hum is the transport layer for bee coordination. The bridge forager translates events to chis on the mesh; the chain remains the truth.

## Alternatives considered

A staged Seam B was considered, in which external feeds inform only a "soft signal" surfaced to followers as advisory metadata without entering the slash math. Rejected because the soft-hard line is operationally fragile: any pressure to harden the soft signal moves the architecture toward Seam B without reopening the decision.

## Reference

The decision is preserved on chain by the deployment-time wiring of the `oracle` address and the `universeWhitelist` address. Off-platform feeds cannot be substituted without redeploying the contract.
